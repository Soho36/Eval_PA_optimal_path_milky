LEGACY 25K MILKY-COW COPY-TO-ALL OVERLAY
Version: 2026-09-01.copy-to-all.2
Status: user-authorized project rule; effective for this repository only
Applicability: [-infinity, +infinity) unless a named scenario overrides a field

1. PRECEDENCE AND EVIDENCE

This overlay supersedes only the PA opportunity, routing, inventory, acquisition,
replacement, scaling, and external-capital language inherited from sections 7
and 9A of rules/25k_legacy_rules.txt and the corresponding claims in
rules/LEGACY_25K_AUDIT.md, data/INPUT_CONTRACTS_FROM_PARENT.md, and
config/legacy_25k_baseline.json.

The copied files remain evidence for common Legacy 25K Evaluation, account,
drawdown, fee, activation, and payout candidates. They are not edited and their
recorded source revisions and byte hashes remain authoritative provenance.
For runtime governance, config/milky_cow_contract_gate.json is the active
foundation gate. The transferred config/milky_cow_study_contract.json is a
frozen initial scope record and is not runtime-merged with this gate or with the
parent baseline.

The explicit user instruction and this overlay control the new PA-book
semantics. Code, tests, manifests, and fixtures establish what inherited
artifacts actually do; they do not silently override this overlay. Any active
implementation conflict with an effective project rule stops the affected
modeling work.

2. GLOBAL COMPLETED-TRADE OPPORTUNITY STREAM

Normalize and verify all 12,658 RR1 raw offers before selection. Sort with the
causal key (entry_at, window_order, source_row, ticket). Select one global stream
with one position slot and [entry_at, exit_at) occupancy. An exit at T frees the
slot for an entry at T. Selection is performed once on the frozen tape, outside
every PA account and book-size scenario; changing the number or state of PAs
must never change the accepted global opportunity stream.

The audited 9,299 accepted and 3,359 blocked counts are parity targets derived
from the verified input, not hard-coded filters. A count mismatch stops affected
modeling work. A PA copy decision must carry an accepted-opportunity record
bound to the selector ID, accepted-stream digest, ordinal, and raw-offer count;
a bare or blocked raw offer is not a valid PA-book input.

An integrated lifecycle must additionally bind each accepted-opportunity record
to the exact verified selection row at that ordinal and consume rows
monotonically. Re-selecting a blocked offer on a singleton or cycle-local subset
does not create valid PA evidence. It must settle the prior PA copy batch before
entering the next bound opportunity. The RR1 tape contains one accepted
zero-duration opportunity. At its timestamp, settle earlier positive-duration
PA and Evaluation exits first; then enter its Evaluation copy, enter its PA
copy batch, settle the PA batch, and settle the Evaluation copy, all before
payouts, renewals, activations, purchases, or positive-duration entries at that
timestamp. The zero-duration opportunity cannot affect its own entry eligibility.

This section locks the PA-phase opportunity source. Whether the integrated
Evaluation phase consumes this already-selected stream or retains the pinned
per-Evaluation/per-renewal fill-time adapter is unresolved. The copied EODMAE
fixture may be executed only as an upstream behavior lock until that choice is
made.

3. COPY-TO-ALL PA DISTRIBUTION

For each accepted global opportunity, create one account-level trade copy for
every eligible PA. A PA is eligible exactly when it is activated, alive, active,
and its activation timestamp is strictly earlier than the opportunity entry.
Every live activated PA is active; there is no standby, dormant, reserve, or
later-promotion role.

There is no router, selection competition, requested-copy R, max_headroom order,
K/S inventory, seat fill rate, congestion loss, or busy-account capacity metric.
Deterministic PA-ID ordering is permitted only for reproducible reporting and
event processing; it must not change membership in the recipient set.

Because the input stream has one global [entry_at, exit_at) position, a normal
PA cannot be busy when the next accepted entry occurs. A rule or compliance
check may block an individual account copy only if that check is explicitly
configured and the block is recorded with a reason. Absent such a rule, the
account-copy count equals the eligible active-PA count. The resulting copy batch
is an outstanding event record until the source trade's exact exit timestamp.
It may settle once; it is not a busy-seat, routing, or fill-rate mechanic.

4. ACCOUNT DIVERGENCE AND CORRELATION

Copies use the same one-MNQ source P&L, MAE, MFE, timestamps, and path scenario.
Before scaling, execution assumptions, activation times, account balances,
payout histories, or prior deaths diverge, identical PAs must remain state-
identical and must survive or die together. The model must report clustered
deaths rather than collapse them into one book-level death. Because the source
tape supplies only completed-trade extrema, an intratrade liquidation is
observed at the exit event and must not be reported as if the source completed
P&L were realized. Whether round-turn commission is included at MAE/MFE equity
or only at close remains an explicit, unresolved PA accounting input.

Completed-trade path order is a named lifecycle-wide uncertainty dimension.
One-sided cases and two-sided cases ending at MAE retain their settlement-
effective order in every scenario. A close at MFE does not exclude an earlier
MFE-to-MAE excursion and remains ambiguous. Ambiguous cases vary across
`source_constrained_then_mae_first`,
`source_constrained_then_mfe_first`, and
`source_constrained_then_seeded_coin` arms. The seeded arm is a reproducible
imputation, not observed truth. Apply the same scenario arm to Evaluation and PA
settlement, while reporting ambiguity encounters separately for their selected
consumer streams. Reports must show all three arms and changes from the seeded
arm in Evaluation passes, failures, time and cost as well as PA deaths and
payouts.

5. MNQ SCALING

Each account copy carries a positive integer MNQ count selected causally from an
explicit scaling schedule at entry. Threshold boundaries, the balance/equity
basis, per-account versus synchronized-book scope, synchronized aggregation,
maximum contracts, and threshold timing must all be named inputs. A threshold
comparison uses only state known before that entry, after earlier same-timestamp
events under the versioned event order.

No integrated run may infer thresholds from max_headroom, K, parent rankings, or
the undated four-mini excerpt. Linear multiplication of one-MNQ P&L/MAE/MFE and
execution costs is also an explicit study assumption and must receive aggregate-
size/slippage stress. The initial schedule and scope remain unresolved.

6. ACQUISITION AND REPLACEMENT

All activated, alive PAs trade; acquiring a dormant spare is prohibited. A PA
can arise only from a paid Evaluation, pass, paid activation, and the locked
activation-time exclusion. A dead PA cannot reset and no replacement PA appears
instantly. Replacement creates an Evaluation/acquisition obligation whose delay,
cash funding, failure, renewal, pass, and activation remain observable.

An active-count target may constrain active PAs plus capacity commitments
(running Evaluations and paid-or-unpaid pending activations) to prevent accidental
overshoot. It is not a router capacity K. Exact acquisition and dead-PA
replacement policy candidates remain unresolved.

For an Evaluation-pipeline replacement trace, each PA death remains an
unprocessed replacement obligation until one funded Evaluation commitment
represents it. If acquisition or concurrency caps admit only part of a
correlated-death cluster, the remainder persists and cannot be relabeled as
growth. That backlog does not pause the already funded Evaluation pipeline or
the exogenous PA opportunity stream.

For the headline 1-through-20 axis, N is the maintained target number of active
PAs. A cohort starts with zero Evaluations and zero PAs, acquires every PA through
the Evaluation/pass/paid-activation lifecycle, attempts to restore deaths under
the selected replacement policy, and never permits active PAs plus running
Evaluations plus pending activations to exceed N. A separately labeled initial-N
active-book run is a diagnostic counterfactual that isolates PA operating
economics; it is not the headline acquisition-from-zero estimand.

7. EXTERNAL CAPITAL AND TREASURY

Every external contribution must be explicit, timestamped, purpose-tagged, and
included in cash reconciliation. No fee may receive a silent top-up. A policy
must state whether outside capital is prohibited, capped by a numeric lifetime
budget, or bridged for explicitly named lifecycle obligations and must state the
irreversible bridge-closing event. The copied through_first_pa convention is a
candidate, not a silently inherited baseline for this project.

8. SWEEP GATE

An integrated parameter sweep is prohibited until executable tests lock:

- verified RR1 normalization and the global accepted-stream parity;
- N eligible PAs producing exactly N copies for N = 1, 2, and 3;
- activation-at-entry exclusion and correlated pre-divergence outcomes;
- every selected scaling threshold, boundary, scope, and timing rule;
- acquisition commitments without dormant PAs or target overshoot;
- death clustering and non-instant replacement through Evaluations;
- external-capital authorization, exhaustion, closure, and reconciliation;
- the selected Evaluation adapter and its parity fixture; and
- deterministic event ordering and provenance.

The eventual policy space includes every integer active-PA target from 1 through
20 and all six payout-policy candidates. No active count, payout policy, scaling
schedule, acquisition policy, or replacement policy is optimal at this stage.
